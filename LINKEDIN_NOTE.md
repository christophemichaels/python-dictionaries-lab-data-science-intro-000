# What I did with the book (LinkedIn version, under 3,000 characters)

I wrote the book first.

In July I finished The Grammar of Things, eight volumes that start with a house on fire and a circle of rocks and rest on one sentence: a thing is a collection of interrelated patterns, persisting across a slice of time, however brief. On pages 33 and 34 I set the primes and the zeros of the zeta function side by side and called them two descriptions of one object, the records and the rule, neither derivable without the other. I wrote it as a parable. I was describing a border.

Then I went into the mathematics to win. I measured the Weil form's floor past the certified range. I drew every prime gap to ten to the eleventh as a chain of triangles and found the rhythm in them. I drew the cone: half-width as time, the prime line as space, the edge where each prime power switches on. I wrote the Möbius energy as a ledger of lifetimes and paid every family I could. I rearranged the remainder a hundred exact ways, every term kept, and every way ended at the same signed comparison, one step from done. All of it under the discipline the book had set before I started: every claim tagged, every receipt kept, "done" never written as a bare word.

The end was the cone. Inside the edge, the stones: every verified fact. Above the edge, everything the stones leave open. One night I said out loud that in every spot of it there is not the zeta zero, only an infinite potentiality defined by the primes themselves. Two days later that was a definition. A week later it was a corollary of a theorem about what finite verification can do, which is nothing: the totality of checkable arithmetic facts adds no theorem to any theory, and the Riemann Hypothesis is the single universal sentence that no record of its instances reaches.

So I arrived back at the book. The coda, written in July, says a billion stones perfectly placed honor a rule and bring it not one inch closer, because the distance was never a distance. It is a border between jurisdictions, not crossed by accumulation. In October that sentence became a theorem.

What is proved: over Peano arithmetic, the Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to its own consistency with the bare axioms of successor, addition and multiplication. It rests on the existence of itself. What I conjecture, under my name, with its consequences proved: that it cannot be derived from within the arithmetic it constrains. If I am right it is true, every instance is a theorem, the sentence is not, and whatever closes it is an axiom from above. Asking the primes to prove it is asking a body to step outside itself to watch itself stand.

I did not prove the Riemann Hypothesis. No one has. I placed the stones well, kept the receipts, and found the border exactly where the book had drawn it. The stones do not fail the circle. They answer to it.

The paper is called RH on a G String. G for Gödel.
