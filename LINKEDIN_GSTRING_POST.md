RH on a G String. G for Gödel.

Nine pages. Here is what they prove, and what they do not.

The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to a single sentence of arithmetic of the form "for every n, a certain finite computation succeeds." Its instances are the local facts: the zeros computed to height three trillion, the certified floors of the Weil form, the pattern checked to any bound. That much is a known theorem (Davis, Matijasevič, Robinson, 1976). What follows from it is the paper.

Theorem 4.1. Finite verification is deductively inert. Add every finitely checkable fact about the integers, all of them at once, to Peano arithmetic or to set theory, and you get no new theorem. The pattern up to any bound is already a theorem of the weakest arithmetic there is. The Riemann Hypothesis lives in the one unbounded quantifier, and nothing a computation can deliver carries it.

Theorem 5.1. Over Peano arithmetic, the Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to its own consistency with the bare axioms of successor, addition and multiplication. Not implied by, not implies. Equivalent. It is true exactly if no finite derivation from the minimal arithmetic ever reaches a counterexample, and arithmetic can see that this is so. A refutation would be a single integer. If it cannot be refuted, it is true.

Theorem 6.1. Any principle that establishes it is a universal law, not a record. It cannot be a verification or a sum of verifications, and whatever else it says, it proves a consistency statement. Hilbert's word for that was a consistency proof. A framework that explains why the entire pattern holds is, formally, exactly that.

Then the mathematics I spent the year on: Weil positivity on every support, subpower Möbius energy, the record estimate, the one-sided Möbius bound, the lifetime census. Each one is, in set theory, the same statement as the consistency of the Riemann Hypothesis with arithmetic. That is why every exact rearrangement ended at an equivalence. An identity is a biconditional, and biconditionals move nothing. The one certified cone of primes provably leaves the zeros undetermined beyond its horizon.

Then the conjecture, under my name, with its consequences proved: that no proof lies within set theory at all. If I am right, the hypothesis is true, every instance is a theorem, the sentence is not, and whatever closes it is an axiom from above. The only case ever closed, curves over finite fields, was closed by exactly such an axiom, the Hodge index theorem, supplied by a geometry that exists there and is not known to exist for the rational numbers.

I did not prove the Riemann Hypothesis. The paper says so on its first page. What it determines is the form any proof must take, and it files my belief as what it is. The human version was written three months earlier, in The Grammar of Things: a billion stones perfectly placed honor a rule and bring it not one inch closer. Borders of that kind are not crossed by accumulation.

The paper asks to be audited. Every theorem is proved on the page or cited to its source.
