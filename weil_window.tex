\begin{abstract}
Let $\lambda(a)$ be the floor of the odd-sector Weil form over test functions supported in $[-a,a]$. Weil's criterion, in Yoshida's odd-test form, says that $\lambda(a)\ge 0$ for every $a$ is equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis; the floor is certified positive to $a=0.8$ (Zhu) and numerically decays doubly exponentially in $a$. We study the floor as a function of the window. Two results are exact. First, the archimedean part of the form on a window is one half of the Dirichlet form of the logarithmic Laplacian plus a bounded form, with an explicit confining wall $\tfrac12\log(1/(a-|y|))$ at the edge; by the boundary theory of the logarithmic Laplacian, a bounded minimizer is bounded by $C_0(\log(a/\delta))^{-1/2}$ at distance $\delta$ from the edge, and numerically it behaves like $C(a)(\log(a/\delta)+\beta)^{-1/2}$ with an amplitude $C(a)$ that we measure on meshes graded to $10^{-12}$ of the edge. Consequently the entry of a prime power into the window produces no kink in $\lambda$ but a soft kink, $\lambda'$ continuous with an increment of order $C^2/\log(1/\varepsilon)$ at scale $\varepsilon$, which we verify over four decades. Second, between entries the floor obeys the sliding-window identity
\[
a\,\lambda'(a) \;=\; -\Big[\,D_\infty(f)+D_P(f)+2P(f)^2+2P(f)M(f)\Big],
\]
proved for the $K$-mode form and, for the exact form, as a two-sided Dini-derivative statement holding at every point of differentiability, where $f$ is the minimizer, $D_\infty$ is the archimedean dilation form, whose symbol tends to $1$, $D_P(f)=-\sum_n 2\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\log n\; g_f'(\log n)$ is the Euler product differentiated against the derivative of the autocorrelation of $f$ at the prime-power positions, and $P,M$ are the polar moments. We evaluate its three terms in ball arithmetic on the $K$-mode minimizer and confirm the identity independently on the Fourier side; three order-one quantities cancel to the size of $\lambda$. The floor is continuous and non-increasing, and under a bounded-variation hypothesis on near-minimizers it is locally Lipschitz; every minimizer computed, on meshes graded to $10^{-12}$ of the edge and with up to $160$ modes, has total variation exactly $4\sup|f|$, being unimodal on each half window, so the regularity clause of the conjecture below reduces to that hypothesis. A third statement is numerical: on seven supports, with and without primes, $\lambda'(a)=-2C(a)^2$ to a part in a thousand (an eighth, at an entry, to $1.7\%$), the Hadamard formula one expects for this window problem, so that $\Phi'=2C^2/\lambda$ with $\Phi=-\log\lambda$. The derivative of the floor is a property of the ground state alone: the pencil $(-Q',Q)$ has eigenvalues of size $6\times10^{10}\,\Tst$ at $a=1$ while the minimizer's direction gives $3.7\,\Tst$, so no inequality between the two forms can hold. With the horizon $\Tst(a)=2\pi e^{2a}$, finite-mode upper bounds on the floor from height $17$ to height $130$ (down to $\lambda\approx10^{-93}$, with up to $230$ modes and rigorous enclosures of the truncated matrix's lowest eigenvalue beyond $a=1$) give $\Phi'/\Tst$ rising from $3.2$ to $3.9$ on coarse grids, and the Hellmann--Feynman derivative resolves a sawtooth at every prime-power entry: a rise into the entry, of $10$ to $45\%$ over the last $0.003$, as the form without the entering prime heads toward indefiniteness, a drop across it, and a relaxation after, with peaks $3.3$ at the entry of $2$ and $4.1$--$4.3$ at the entries of $3,4,5,7$. We state the bounded-relay conjecture, $\lambda$ locally absolutely continuous with $\lambda'\ge-c\,\Tst\lambda$ almost everywhere, which implies the Riemann Hypothesis by Gr\"onwall's inequality from Zhu's certified support, and the decay law $\Phi'/\Tst\to c_0$; the data exclude the raw phase-space count, disfavour the logarithmic law and the derivative of Zhu's even-sector Landau--Widom law, and favour a constant near $3.8$. Evaluated on the first $6000$ zeros of $\zeta$, the sum of $|F(\gamma)|^2$ over the zeros returns the floor of the $K$-mode minimizer to $3\times10^{-4}$ or better at $a=0.6,0.8,1.0$, down to $\lambda=10^{-26}$, and the zero-side dilation sum returns its derivative to $3\times10^{-3}$ or better: the explicit formula closes on the actual zeros for both, a third and independent measurement of the identity after the matrices and the Fourier symbol. The zeros below the horizon carry at most $10^{-5}$ of the floor, the first zero $10^{-24}$ of it at $a=1$, and the floor is made by the zeros between one and a half and three horizons. The fraction of the floor carried by the zeros above height $T$ is $\Phi'/(\pi T)$ from a few horizons on, on every minimizer computed (edge-FEM to $1\%$ over two decades of height, $K$-mode to $1$--$3\%$ down to $\lambda=10^{-93}$): the rate of decay of the floor is $\pi$ times the effective height of the zeros that carry it, $\Phi'=\pi T_{\mathrm{eff}}$, and the two conjectures say that this height is tied to the horizon. Under the Riemann Hypothesis the floor is a sampling constant for the zeros above the horizon, and a prolate-type count bounds $\Phi$ below by about half of what is observed. Nothing in this paper proves the Riemann Hypothesis. Theorems, computations with their precision, and conjectures are distinguished throughout.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Status conventions}
A \textbf{Theorem}, \textbf{Proposition} or \textbf{Lemma} without citation is proved in the text. A statement with a citation is taken from the literature and its hypotheses are stated. A \textbf{Computation} is a numerical result with its method, precision and cross-checks; a finite-mode minimum is a minimum over a subspace and hence an upper bound on the floor, and every such value is labelled by its mode count $K$; derivatives of finite-mode floors are Hellmann--Feynman derivatives of the $K$-mode form and are called so, never ``exact''. A \textbf{Conjecture} is a precise statement with the evidence for it. A \textbf{Heuristic} or \textbf{Remark} makes no mathematical claim. The hypothesis that a minimizer is bounded, needed for the edge law, is written (H$_\infty$); the hypothesis that near-minimizers have bounded sup norm times total variation, which gives Lipschitz continuity of the floor, is written (H$_{\mathrm{BV}}$); both are carried explicitly wherever they are used.

\section{The form, the floor and the horizon}\label{sec:form}

For $f\in L^2(\R)$ real with compact support write $F=\hat f$, $F(t)=\int f(x)e^{itx}\,dx$, and $g=f\star\tilde f$, $g(u)=\int f(x)f(x-u)\,dx$, so that $\hat g=|F|^2$. The Weil form in the normalization used throughout is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q}
Q(f) \;=\; W_\infty(g)\;-\;\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\big(g(\log n)+g(-\log n)\big)\;+\;\big(\hat g(i/2)+\hat g(-i/2)\big),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Winf}
W_\infty(g) \;=\; -(\gamma+\log\pi)\,g(0)\;+\;2\int_0^\infty \frac{g(0)e^{-2x}-g(x)e^{-x/2}}{1-e^{-2x}}\,dx .
\end{equation}
By the explicit formula \cite{weil}, $Q(f)=\sum_\rho \hat g\big((\rho-\tfrac12)/i\big)$ over the nontrivial zeros; on the geometric side, in Fourier form,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symbol}
Q(f)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R |F(t)|^2\,\Psi(t)\,dt \;+\;\text{polar},\qquad
\Psi(t)=\Ree\psi\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}{2}\big)-\log\pi-\sum_{n}\frac{2\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\cos(t\log n),
\end{equation}
where the polar term is $2F(i/2)^2$ for even $f$ and $-2P(f)^2$, $P(f)=\int f(x)\sinh(x/2)\,dx$, for odd $f$; the multiplier $\Ree\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2)-\log\pi$ is that of $W_\infty$ by the integral representation of $\psi$. This is the normalization of Zhu \cite{zhu} and of Checkpoints 15--20 of \cite{michaels-atlas}; Computation~\ref{comp:zhu} confirms the identification numerically.

\begin{definition}
For $a>0$ the \emph{odd floor} is
\[
\lambda(a)=\inf\{\,Q(f):\ f \text{ odd},\ \operatorname{supp}f\subset[-a,a],\ \|f\|_2=1,\ Q(f)<\infty\,\},
\]
$\Phi(a)=-\log\lambda(a)$ wherever $\lambda(a)>0$, and the \emph{horizon} is $\Tst(a)=2\pi e^{2a}$. $N(T)$ denotes the number of zeros with $0<\gamma\le T$.
\end{definition}

Since $g$ is supported in $[-2a,2a]$, only the prime powers with $\log n<2a$ enter $Q(f)$; the \emph{entries} are $a_n=\tfrac12\log n$. The form $Q$ itself does not depend on $a$, so $\lambda$ is non-increasing.

\begin{theorem}[Weil \cite{weil}; Yoshida \cite{yoshida}, Prop.\ 1, as recorded in Suzuki \cite{suzuki-screw}]\label{thm:weil}
The Riemann Hypothesis holds if and only if $Q(f)\ge0$ for every compactly supported smooth $f$; and $Q(f)\ge0$ for every odd such $f$ already implies it. Hence $\mathrm{RH}\iff \lambda(a)\ge0$ for all $a>0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Zhu \cite{zhu}, Theorem 6.2]\label{thm:zhu}
For $a=0.8$, in the odd sector, $8.206\times10^{-15}\le\lambda(0.8)\le 2.347\times10^{-14}$, certified by interval arithmetic (and in the even sector $8.9\times10^{-18}\le\lambda^{\mathrm{even}}(0.8)\le2.523\times10^{-16}$). In particular $\lambda(a)>0$ for $0<a\le0.8$.
\end{theorem}

The horizon is the height at which the zeros become too dense for the window. A function of exponential type $a$ has zeros of upper density at most $a/\pi$ (Jensen's formula; see Boas \cite{boas}); the zeros of $\zeta$ have density $(2\pi)^{-1}\log(t/2\pi)$ at height $t$, which equals $a/\pi$ exactly at $t=\Tst(a)$. Zhu's resolution threshold, $2\pi e^{(4+o(1))e^{a}}$ for a one-stroke positivity certificate at half-width $a$ \cite{zhu}, is a different and far larger scale. Everything below is organized around the pair $(\lambda(a),\Tst(a))$.

\begin{lemma}[existence, monotonicity, continuity, differentiability a.e.]\label{lem:exist}
For every $a>0$ the infimum defining $\lambda(a)$ is attained, the infimum over odd $C_c^\infty(-a,a)$ functions is the same number, $\lambda$ is non-increasing and continuous, and $\lambda$ is differentiable at almost every $a$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:wall} and the discussion after it, $Q$ on $L^2_{\mathrm{odd}}(-a,a)$ is one half of the Dirichlet form of $L_\Delta$ plus a form bounded on $L^2$; the form domain $\{f:\int\log(2+|t|)|F|^2<\infty\}$ embeds compactly into $L^2(-a,a)$ \cite[Thm.\ 2.1 ff.]{chen-weth}, so $Q$ is bounded below and lower semicontinuous on the unit sphere and a minimizing sequence has a convergent subsequence. Density of $C_c^\infty$ in the form domain gives the second claim. Monotonicity is inclusion of the admissible sets, and a monotone function is differentiable almost everywhere. Continuity: for $a<a'$ and a minimizer $f'$ at $a'$, the dilate $f'_{a/a'}$ (notation of Section~\ref{sec:identity}) is admissible at $a$, so $0\le\lambda(a)-\lambda(a')\le Q(f'_{a/a'})-Q(f')$. Minimizers at supports in $[a,a+1]$ have form norm bounded in terms of $a$ and $\lambda(a)$ alone (the wall is positive, the prime and polar terms are bounded on the unit sphere), and on a form-bounded set $Q(f_b)-Q(f)\to0$ uniformly as $b\to1$: in the archimedean term because $|\Psi_\infty(s/b)-\Psi_\infty(s)|\le w_*|\log b|$ with $w_*=\sup_t|t\Psi_\infty'(t)|<\infty$, in the prime terms because $|g_f(u)-g_f(u')|\le\|f\|\,\|f(\cdot-u)-f(\cdot-u')\|$ and $\|f(\cdot-h)-f\|^2\le h^2T^2\|f\|^2+4\int_{|t|>T}|F|^2$ is uniformly small on a form-bounded set, in the polar term because $P$ is a bounded functional. Letting $a'\downarrow a$ gives right continuity; left continuity is $\lambda(a')\le Q(f_{a'/a})\to Q(f)=\lambda(a)$ as $a'\uparrow a$, for a minimizer $f$ at $a$. In particular the floor cannot jump.
\end{proof}

\section{Three engines}\label{sec:engines}

All computations use the orthonormal odd Legendre basis on the window, $f(y)=a^{-1/2}\sum_{i<K}c_iN_iP_{2i+1}(y/a)$, $N_i=\sqrt{(4i+3)/2}$, in which the matrix $Q_K(a)$ of \eqref{eq:Q} is assembled from the exact cross-correlations $g_{ij}(x)=\int\phi_i(u)\phi_j(u-x)\,du$ by Gauss--Legendre rules exact for the polynomial products. The \emph{$K$-mode floor} $\lambda_K(a)$ is the lowest eigenvalue of $Q_K(a)$; it is an upper bound on $\lambda(a)$, and $\lambda_1$ below denotes the second eigenvalue. Three implementations are used, all in the repository accompanying this paper \cite{michaels-repo}.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \texttt{rh\_weil\_odd.py}: mpmath, 50--60 digits, $K\le72$, $80$ outer quadrature nodes. Reproduces the floors and the failure points of \cite{michaels-folds} to 4--6 digits. Its outer rule is exact only to polynomial degree $159$, which costs $10^{-4}$ in relative accuracy at $K=72$.
\item \texttt{rh\_weil\_arb.py}: ball arithmetic (python-flint 0.9.0 over FLINT/Arb), $K\le230$, 500--800 bits, with certified Gauss--Legendre rules, $2K+8$ inner and $2K+100$ outer nodes, and a rigorous enclosure of the lowest eigenvalue of the assembled matrix. The Legendre recurrence is evaluated with a midpoint reset at each degree, since its ball version doubles the radius per degree; the enclosure therefore covers the assembly and the eigenvalue computation but not the polynomial evaluations, which are 500-bit floating point, nor the truncation of the outer rule. It reproduces the mpmath floors to twenty digits where both are converged and runs a $180$-mode point in six minutes.
\item \texttt{rh\_edge\_fem.py}: odd-extended hat functions on a mesh of the half window graded geometrically to $10^{-12}a$ (350--490 nodes; the ``two-stage'' mesh has ratio $0.9$ from $0.05a$ to $10^{-4}a$ and $\tfrac12$ below), exact cross-correlations, closed-form archimedean tails, double precision. Built to read the minimizer at the edge, where a Legendre truncation resolves only $\delta\gtrsim a/K^2$. Its amplitudes carry a mesh bias of $10^{-3}$ (prime-free) to $10^{-2}$ (full form).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{computation}\label{comp:zhu}
$\lambda_{40}(0.8)=1.5935\times10^{-14}$ and $\lambda_{64}(0.8)=1.5659\times10^{-14}$, both inside Zhu's certified odd interval of Theorem~\ref{thm:zhu}, which fixes the normalization and shows the truncation error at that support. At $a=0.3$, where no prime is inside the window, $\lambda_{64}(0.3)=0.2225569$ and the edge-FEM floor $0.2225566$ agree to $1.3\times10^{-6}$; at $a_3=\tfrac12\log3$ the $64$-mode profile and the FEM profile agree to $10^{-4}$ for $|y|\le0.99a$.
\end{computation}

\section{The archimedean wall and the edge law}\label{sec:edge}

\begin{proposition}[decomposition on the window]\label{prop:wall}
Let $J_\Gamma(s)=e^{-|s|/2}/(1-e^{-2|s|})$, $\mathcal J(x)=\int_x^\infty J_\Gamma=\operatorname{artanh}(e^{-x/2})+\arctan(e^{-x/2})$, and $c_1=-(\gamma+\log\pi)-\tfrac{\pi}{2}-3\log2$. For $f$ supported in $[-a,a]$,
\[
W_\infty(g_f)=c_1\|f\|^2+\tfrac12\iint_{[-a,a]^2}J_\Gamma(u-v)|f(u)-f(v)|^2\,du\,dv+\int_{-a}^a V_a(y)|f(y)|^2\,dy,\qquad
V_a(y)=\mathcal J(a-y)+\mathcal J(a+y),
\]
and $V_a(y)=\tfrac12\log\frac1{a-|y|}+\kappa_a+o(1)$ as $|y|\to a$, with $\kappa_a=\tfrac12\log4+\tfrac\pi4+\mathcal J(2a)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With $g(0)-g(x)=\tfrac12\int|f(u)-f(u-x)|^2du$, the integral in \eqref{eq:Winf} is $\tfrac12\iint_{\R^2}J_\Gamma(u-v)|f(u)-f(v)|^2$, and $2\int_0^\infty(e^{-2x}-e^{-x/2})/(1-e^{-2x})\,dx=\psi(\tfrac14)+\gamma=-\pi/2-3\log2$ by the integral representation of $\psi$. Splitting the double integral according to whether $v$ lies in the window gives the three terms, the two cross-regions contributing $\tfrac12\int V_a|f|^2$ each. The substitution $w=e^{-s/2}$ gives $\int_x^\infty J_\Gamma=\int_0^{e^{-x/2}}2\,dw/(1-w^4)=\mathcal J(x)$, and $\operatorname{artanh}(e^{-x/2})=\tfrac12\log(4/x)+o(1)$ gives the constant; at $a=a_3$, $\kappa_a=2.660623$, and the direct evaluation of $V_a-\tfrac12\log(1/\delta)$ at $\delta=10^{-9}$ gives $2.6606231$.
\end{proof}

The symbol of the whole-line form $\tfrac12\iint J_\Gamma|f(u)-f(v)|^2$ is $\sigma(\xi)=\int J_\Gamma(s)(1-\cos s\xi)\,ds=\log|\xi|+O(1)$, so on the window the archimedean form is one half of the Dirichlet form $\int\log(\xi^2)|\hat f|^2$ of the logarithmic Laplacian $L_\Delta$ of Chen--Weth \cite{chen-weth} plus a bounded quadratic form; the prime terms are bounded translation operators and the polar term has rank one. Write $Q(f)=\tfrac12\mathcal E_L(f)+\ip{Bf}{f}$ with $B$ bounded and self-adjoint on $L^2(-a,a)$ (its kernel is bounded on the window). The weak Euler--Lagrange equation of a minimizer is then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:EL}
L_\Delta f = 2(\lambda-B)f\quad\text{in }(-a,a),\qquad f=0\ \text{outside},
\end{equation}
tested against $C_c^\infty(-a,a)$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Hypothesis (H$_\infty$).} The minimizer $f$ is bounded. \emph{This is not known to follow from the form domain, which embeds in no $L^p$, $p>2$; an $L^\infty$ bound of De Giorgi--Moser type for $L_\Delta$ with a bounded lower-order perturbation would supply it. Numerically $\max|f|=1.37$ at $a_3$ and $1.61$ at $a=0.3$.}

\begin{theorem}[edge law; Chen--Weth \cite{chen-weth}, Thm.\ 1.11; Hern\'andez-Santamar\'ia--L\'opez R\'ios--Salda\~na \cite{hls}, Thm.\ 1.1]\label{thm:edge}
Assume (H$_\infty$). Then $f$ is continuous on $\R$ and there is $C_0$ with
\[
|f(y)|\le C_0\,\big(\log\tfrac1{a-|y|}\big)^{-1/2}\qquad(|y|<a,\ a-|y|<0.1).
\]
In particular $f(\pm a)=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By \eqref{eq:EL} and (H$_\infty$), $f\in H(\Omega)\cap L^\infty(\R)$ is a weak solution of $L_\Delta f=h$ with $h=2(\lambda-B)f\in L^\infty(\Omega)$, $\Omega=(-a,a)$, which is a Lipschitz domain with the uniform exterior sphere condition. \cite[Thm.\ 1.11]{chen-weth} gives continuity and the rate $(\log(1/\delta))^{-\tau}$ for every $\tau<\tfrac12$; \cite[Thm.\ 1.1]{hls} gives $\tau=\tfrac12$, with $\ell(r)=-1/\ln\min\{0.1,r\}$.
\end{proof}

The exponent $\tfrac12$ is sharp for the torsion function of a small interval \cite[Thm.\ 1.2]{hls}, and a Hopf-type lemma gives the rate from below for nonnegative supersolutions \cite[Thm.\ 1.4]{hls}; the minimizer here is odd, so the lemma does not apply to it directly and the lower rate is a numerical statement (Computation~\ref{comp:edge}).

\begin{heuristic}\label{heur:edge}
In $t=\log(1/\delta)$, $\delta=a-y$, equation \eqref{eq:EL} near the edge has the shape $t\,h(t)-\tfrac12\int_0^t h(\tau)\,d\tau=O(h)+O(1)$, the coefficient $t$ being $\tfrac12$ from the wall and $\tfrac12$ from the interior kernel, and the nonlocal term coming from the long-range part of the kernel, which in the variable $t$ is the constant $\tfrac12$ on one side; its solutions are $h=Ct^{-1/2}(1+O(1/t))$. We have not derived the equation in this form.
\end{heuristic}

Checkpoint 17 of \cite{michaels-atlas} applied Theorem~\ref{thm:edge} to the forced solutions of the same operator and left open the existence of the normalized trace $\lim\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}\,f(a-\delta)$; the following computation is the numerical answer for the ground state.

\section{The relay is a soft kink}\label{sec:kink}

At an entry $a_n$ the new term $-2\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}g(\log n)$ vanishes and grows with $a$.

\begin{proposition}[$K$-mode kink]\label{prop:kink}
For the $K$-mode form, $\lambda_K$ is continuous at $a_n$ and $\lambda_K'(a_n^+)-\lambda_K'(a_n^-)=4\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}f_K(a_n)^2$, where $f_K$ is the normalized $K$-mode minimizer at entry.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $a=a_n+\varepsilon$ and odd $f$, $g(\log n)=\int_{a-2\varepsilon}^{a}f(y)f(y-\log n)\,dy=-\int_0^{2\varepsilon}f(a-\delta)f(a-2\varepsilon+\delta)\,d\delta$, which is $-2\varepsilon f(a)^2+O(\varepsilon^2)$ for a function continuous at the edge; the $K$-mode minimizer is a polynomial. Hellmann--Feynman with the minimizer frozen gives the jump.
\end{proof}

\begin{computation}[the $K$-mode kink]\label{comp:kink}
With $24$ modes at the entry of $3$, the measured jump is $0.948$ of the prediction $2.276\times10^{-4}$ at step $5\times10^{-4}$ and $0.9998$ at step $10^{-5}$ (\texttt{rh\_kink\_steps.py} in \cite{michaels-repo}); the deficit at coarser steps is the reorganization of the minimizer within the scale $\text{gap}/\|Q_K'\|\approx3\times10^{-5}$, the ratio of the spectral gap $\lambda_1-\lambda_K$ to the norm of the entering term's derivative.
\end{computation}

For the exact form, under (H$_\infty$), Theorem~\ref{thm:edge} gives $f(a_n)=0$ and the proposition's jump is a quantity of the truncation. What replaces it follows from the fitted profile of Computation~\ref{comp:edge}: with $f(a-\delta)=C(\log(a/\delta)+\beta)^{-1/2}$, the product integral is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:soft}
-2\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}g(\log n)=\frac{4\Lambda(n)C^2}{\sqrt n}\,\frac{\varepsilon}{\log(a/\varepsilon)+\beta+1-\log2}\,\Big(1+O\big(\log^{-2}(1/\varepsilon)\big)\Big),
\end{equation}
so $\lambda'$ is continuous across the entry and its increment at scale $\varepsilon$ is $4\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}C^2/(\log(a/\varepsilon)+\beta+1-\log2)$: a soft kink, vanishing only logarithmically. (An earlier version omitted the constant $1-\log2$ in the denominator, which is an $8\%$ effect at $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$.)

\section{The sliding-window identity}\label{sec:identity}

For $f$ supported in $[-a,a]$ and $b>0$ let $f_b(x)=b^{-1/2}f(x/b)$, supported in $[-ab,ab]$ with $\|f_b\|=\|f\|$. Define, with $F=\hat f$ and $g=g_f$,
\[
D_\infty(f)=\frac1{2\pi}\int|F(t)|^2\,t\,\partial_t\Ree\psi\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2\big)\,dt,\qquad
D_P(f)=-\sum_{\log n<2a}\frac{2\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,\log n\;g'(\log n),
\]
$P(f)=\int f(x)\sinh(x/2)\,dx$, $M(f)=\int f(x)\,x\cosh(x/2)\,dx$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dtilde}
\mathcal D(f)=D_\infty(f)+D_P(f)+2P(f)^2+2P(f)M(f).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[dilation derivative of the form]\label{lem:dil}
Let $a$ lie strictly between two consecutive entries and let $f$ be supported in $[-a,a]$ with $Q(f)<\infty$ and $g_f$ differentiable at every $\log n<2a$. Then $b\mapsto Q(f_b)$ is differentiable at $b=1$ and $\frac{d}{db}Q(f_b)\big|_{b=1}=-\mathcal D(f)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\widehat{f_b}(t)=\sqrt b\,F(bt)$ exactly, jumps included. In \eqref{eq:Q}, $g_{f_b}(u)=g_f(u/b)$, so the prime terms are $-2\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}g_f(\log n/b)$, differentiable at $b=1$ with derivative $2\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\log n\,g_f'(\log n)=$ the $n$-th term of $-D_P(f)$; for $b$ near $1$ no new prime enters. The archimedean term is $\frac1{2\pi}\int b|F(bt)|^2\Psi_\infty(t)\,dt=\frac1{2\pi}\int|F(s)|^2\Psi_\infty(s/b)\,ds$ with $\Psi_\infty=\Ree\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2)-\log\pi$; since $t\Psi_\infty'(t)$ is bounded on $\R$, differentiation under the integral is justified by dominated convergence and gives $-\frac1{2\pi}\int|F|^2\,t\Psi_\infty'(t)\,dt=-D_\infty(f)$ at $b=1$. For the polar term, $P(f_b)=\sqrt b\int f(y)\sinh(by/2)\,dy$ gives $\frac{d}{db}P(f_b)|_{b=1}=\tfrac12P(f)+\tfrac12M(f)$, so $\frac{d}{db}(-2P(f_b)^2)|_{b=1}=-2P^2-2PM$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[sliding-window identity, $K$-mode form]\label{thm:identity}
Let $a$ lie strictly between two consecutive entries and let $\lambda_K(a)$ be simple with normalized eigenvector $c$, $f$ the corresponding function. Then $\lambda_K$ is differentiable at $a$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
a\,\lambda_K'(a)=-\mathcal D(f).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The basis functions are a dilation family, $\phi_i^{(ab)}=(\phi_i^{(a)})_b$, so $Q_K(ab)=[Q((\phi_i^{(a)})_b,(\phi_j^{(a)})_b)]_{ij}$ is real-analytic in $b$ near $1$ (each entry is a finite sum of terms as in Lemma~\ref{lem:dil}, with $g_{ij}$ polynomial), Hellmann--Feynman for a simple eigenvalue gives $\frac{d}{db}\lambda_K(ab)|_{b=1}=\ip{c}{\frac{d}{db}Q_K(ab)|_{b=1}c}=\frac{d}{db}Q(f_b)|_{b=1}$ by bilinearity, and Lemma~\ref{lem:dil} evaluates it.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[exact form: two-sided Dini derivatives]\label{prop:dini}
Let $a$ lie strictly between two consecutive entries and let $f$ be a minimizer at support $a$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:exist}) with $g_f$ differentiable at every $\log n<2a$. Then
\[
a\,\Dp\lambda(a)\;\le\;-\mathcal D(f)\;\le\;a\,\Dm\lambda(a),
\]
where $\Dp,\Dm$ are the upper-right and lower-left Dini derivatives; hence $a\lambda'(a)=-\mathcal D(f)$ at every point of differentiability of $\lambda$, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:exist} is almost every $a$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For every $b>0$, $f_b$ is admissible at support $ab$, so $\lambda(ab)\le Q(f_b)$, with equality at $b=1$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:dil}, $Q(f_b)=\lambda(a)-(b-1)\mathcal D(f)+o(b-1)$. For $b>1$, $(\lambda(ab)-\lambda(a))/(b-1)\le-\mathcal D(f)+o(1)$; for $b<1$ the division reverses the inequality. Since $\frac{d}{db}=a\frac{d}{da}$ at $b=1$, the two bounds follow.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The hypothesis on $g_f'$ is implied by $f'\in L^1$, which the edge law $C(\log(1/\delta))^{-1/2}$ suggests but which is not proved; it is the only condition beyond existence. The form-domain condition $\int\log(2+|t|)|F|^2<\infty$ does not give $\int|t||F|^2<\infty$, so the prime part of $\mathcal D$ is to be read in $x$-space, as defined.
\end{remark}

The hypothesis is natural in the class of functions of bounded variation, and in that class the dilation derivative is bounded, which makes the floor Lipschitz.

\begin{lemma}[dilation of a function of bounded variation]\label{lem:bv}
Let $f\in L^\infty(\R)$ have bounded variation $\operatorname{Var}f$ and support in $[-a,a]$. Then $g_f$ is Lipschitz with constant $\|f\|_\infty\operatorname{Var}f$; the map $b\mapsto Q(f_b)$ is locally Lipschitz on $(0,\infty)$, entries included, with $b\,\frac{d}{db}Q(f_b)=-\mathcal D(f_b)$ at every $b$ at which $g_f$ is differentiable at the points $(\log n)/b$; and for every $b$
\[
|\mathcal D(f_b)|\;\le\;w_*\|f\|^2+\frac{\|f\|_\infty\operatorname{Var}f}{b}\sum_{\log n<2ab}\frac{2\Lambda(n)\log n}{\sqrt n}+\big(2\sigma(ab)^2+2\sigma(ab)\mu(ab)\big)\|f\|^2,
\]
where $w_*=\sup_t t\,\partial_t\Ree\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2)=2.0807$, attained at $t=0.520$, $\sigma(a)^2=\int_{-a}^a\sinh^2(x/2)\,dx$ and $\mu(a)^2=\int_{-a}^ax^2\cosh^2(x/2)\,dx$. If $f$ is continuous, $g_f\in C^1$ and $b\mapsto Q(f_b)$ is $C^1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $f$ of bounded variation, $\int|f(y)-f(y-h)|\,dy\le|h|\operatorname{Var}f$, so $|g_f(u)-g_f(u')|\le\|f\|_\infty\int|f(x-u)-f(x-u')|\,dx\le\|f\|_\infty\operatorname{Var}f\,|u-u'|$. The prime terms of $Q(f_b)$ are $-2\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}g_f(\log n/b)$, locally Lipschitz in $b$, with $b\frac{d}{db}g_f(\log n/b)=-(\log n/b)\,g_f'(\log n/b)=-\log n\;g_{f_b}'(\log n)$ wherever $g_f$ is differentiable at $\log n/b$; a term with $\log n=2ab$ enters with the value $g_f(2a)=0$ and continuously, since $g_f$ vanishes on $[2a,\infty)$. The archimedean and polar terms are $C^1$ in $b$ for every $b$, with the derivatives computed in Lemma~\ref{lem:dil}. The bound uses $|t\,\partial_t\Ree\psi|\le w_*$, $|g_{f_b}'|\le\|f_b\|_\infty\operatorname{Var}f_b=b^{-1}\|f\|_\infty\operatorname{Var}f$, and $|P(f_b)|\le\sigma(ab)\|f\|$, $|M(f_b)|\le\mu(ab)\|f\|$ by Cauchy--Schwarz. If $f$ is continuous, $g_f'(u)=-\int f(z+u)\,d(Df)(z)$ with $Df$ the finite derivative measure of $f$, which is continuous in $u$ by dominated convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the floor is Lipschitz under a variation bound]\label{prop:lip}
Let $0<a_0<a_1$ and $M>0$, and assume
\begin{itemize}
\item[(H$_{\mathrm{BV}}$)] for every $a\in[a_0,a_1]$ and $\varepsilon>0$ there is $f\in L^\infty$ of bounded variation, supported in $[-a,a]$, with $\|f\|=1$, $Q(f)\le\lambda(a)+\varepsilon$ and $\|f\|_\infty\operatorname{Var}f\le M$.
\end{itemize}
Then for $a_0\le a<a'\le a_1$,
\[
0\;\le\;\lambda(a)-\lambda(a')\;\le\;L\log\frac{a'}{a}\;\le\;\frac{L}{a_0}\,(a'-a),\qquad
L=w_*+\frac{a_1}{a_0}\,M\sum_{\log n<2a_1}\frac{2\Lambda(n)\log n}{\sqrt n}+2\sigma(a_1)^2+2\sigma(a_1)\mu(a_1).
\]
In particular $\lambda$ is Lipschitz on $[a_0,a_1]$, hence absolutely continuous there, with $\lambda'\ge-L/a_0$ almost everywhere.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The left inequality is Lemma~\ref{lem:exist}. For the right one take $f'$ as in (H$_{\mathrm{BV}}$) at $a'$ and put $b_0=a/a'$. The dilate $f'_{b_0}$ is supported in $[-a,a]$ with unit norm, so $\lambda(a)\le Q(f'_{b_0})$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:bv}, $b\mapsto Q(f'_b)$ is Lipschitz on $[b_0,1]$ with $|b\frac{d}{db}Q(f'_b)|\le L$ almost everywhere (the prime sum runs over $\log n<2a'b\le2a_1$, and $b^{-1}\le a_1/a_0$), so $Q(f'_{b_0})\le Q(f')+L\log(1/b_0)\le\lambda(a')+\varepsilon+L\log(a'/a)$. Let $\varepsilon\to0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:bv}
(H$_{\mathrm{BV}}$) concerns near-minimizers, not the minimizer, and it follows from a bound on the $K$-mode minimizers uniform in $K$: $\lambda(a)=\inf_K\lambda_K(a)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:exist} (the odd Legendre truncations of an odd $C_c^\infty(-a,a)$ function converge to it in the form norm, their jumps at $\pm a$ being of vanishing size and costing $O(\mathrm{jump}^2)$ in a norm that tolerates jumps), so the $K$-mode minimizers are near-minimizers of the exact problem, and they are polynomials, hence of bounded variation. Under (H$_{\mathrm{BV}}$) on every compact subinterval of $[a_0,\infty)$ the regularity clause of Conjecture~\ref{conj:A} holds and the conjecture is the inequality $\lambda'\ge-c\Tst\lambda$ a.e.\ alone. The trivial bound $\lambda'\ge-L/a$ also shows what the conjecture asks: on $[0.6,1]$ with $M=9$ (Computation~\ref{comp:bv}) it gives $L=126$ and $\lambda'\ge-211$, while $\lambda'$ is $-4\times10^{-5}$ at $a=0.6$ and $-3\times10^{-24}$ at $a=1$; the conjecture is that $\mathcal D(f)$, whose three terms are each of order one, cancels to $O(\Tst\lambda)$ on the minimizer, as in \eqref{eq:cancel}.
\end{remark}

\section{The conjectures}\label{sec:conj}

\begin{conjecture}[bounded relay]\label{conj:A}
There are $a_0>0$ and $c>0$ such that $\lambda$ is locally absolutely continuous on $[a_0,\infty)$ and
\[
\lambda'(a)\;\ge\;-c\,\Tst(a)\,\lambda(a)\qquad\text{for almost every }a\ge a_0 .
\]
\end{conjecture}

The statement is meaningful whatever the sign of $\lambda$; absolute continuity is part of the conjecture: Lemma~\ref{lem:exist} gives continuity and differentiability a.e., and Proposition~\ref{prop:lip} gives Lipschitz continuity under (H$_{\mathrm{BV}}$), a bound on the sup norm times the total variation of near-minimizers, which Computation~\ref{comp:bv} supports on every minimizer computed ($\operatorname{Var}f=4\sup|f|\approx6$, converged in the mesh and in $K$). Where $\lambda>0$ it reads $\Phi'\le c\,\Tst$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:AimpliesRH}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:A} with $a_0\le0.8$ implies the Riemann Hypothesis.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $u(a)=\lambda(a)\exp\big(\tfrac c2\Tst(a)\big)$, which is locally absolutely continuous with $u'=e^{c\Tst/2}(\lambda'+c\Tst\lambda)\ge0$ a.e., using $\frac{d}{da}\Tst=2\Tst$. Hence $u$ is non-decreasing and $\lambda(a)\ge\lambda(a_0)\exp\big(-\tfrac c2(\Tst(a)-\Tst(a_0))\big)$ for $a\ge a_0$. With $a_0=0.8$, Theorem~\ref{thm:zhu} gives $\lambda(0.8)\ge8.206\times10^{-15}>0$, so $\lambda>0$ on $[0.8,\infty)$, and $\lambda>0$ on $(0,0.8]$ by the same theorem. Theorem~\ref{thm:weil} concludes.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[decay law]\label{conj:B}
For $a\ge a_0$, $\lambda(a)>0$ and $\Phi'(a)/\Tst(a)$ is bounded above and below by positive constants. In its sharp form, $\Phi'(a)/\Tst(a)\to c_0$, i.e.\ $\lambda(a)=\exp\big(-(c_0/2+o(1))\,2\pi e^{2a}\big)$.
\end{conjecture}

Computation~\ref{comp:grid} supports the weak form from height $17$ to $130$ and, on the odd sector, favours a constant $c_0\approx3.8$ (residual $2.1\%$) over every law with a logarithmic factor; the derivative of Zhu's even-sector law, whose constant $C=2\pi^2$ would give the asymptote $2\pi$, predicts a rise six times larger than observed. Whether the ratio saturates or continues to creep up beyond height $130$ is not decidable from these data. Conjecture~\ref{conj:A} is the one that implies the Riemann Hypothesis; the constant is not its content.

By Theorem~\ref{thm:identity} and Proposition~\ref{prop:dini}, Conjecture~\ref{conj:A} is, at almost every $a$, the inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Aform}
\mathcal D(f)\;\le\;c\,a\,\Tst(a)\,Q(f)\qquad\text{on the minimizer } f,
\end{equation}
and by Computation~\ref{comp:pencil} it holds only there.

\begin{remark}[what a proof cannot be]
Positivity of $Q_{24}$ on a window is destroyed by displacing $\log2$ by $3\times10^{-14}$ at $a=0.8$ and by displacements of order $\exp(-c\Tst(a))$ in general \cite{michaels-folds,michaels-repo}, so no statement about the primes that is invariant under such displacements can imply positivity; and Checkpoint 20 of \cite{michaels-atlas} shows that bounding the reflected prime terms separately from the polar term yields $-\infty$ on translated tests. A proof of Conjecture~\ref{conj:A} must therefore use the exact multiplicative structure of $\Lambda$, and by Computation~\ref{comp:pencil} it must use that $f$ minimizes $Q$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Under the Riemann Hypothesis}\label{sec:rh}

Let $\PW_a$ be the Paley--Wiener space of transforms $F=\hat f$ of $f\in L^2(-a,a)$, here with $f$ odd. If all zeros are real, $Q(f)=\sum_\gamma|F(\gamma)|^2$ (the sum over $\gamma$ with multiplicity, of both signs; it converges when $Q(f)<\infty$), and the dilation computation of Lemma~\ref{lem:dil} on the zero side, $Q(f_b)=\sum_\gamma b|F(b\gamma)|^2$, gives at a point of differentiability
\[
a\lambda'=\lambda+\sum_\gamma\gamma\,\partial_t|F|^2(\gamma)
\]
whenever the last sum converges absolutely, so Conjecture~\ref{conj:A} reads $\sum_\gamma\gamma\,\partial_t|F|^2(\gamma)\ge-(1+ca\Tst)\sum_\gamma|F(\gamma)|^2$: the minimizer is small at the zeros to the same order in its derivative as in its value. And
\[
\lambda(a)=\inf\{\textstyle\sum_\gamma|F(\gamma)|^2:\ F\in\PW_a\ \text{odd},\ \|f\|_2=1\}
\]
is the lower sampling constant of the zeros for $\PW_a$. The zeros have density $(2\pi)^{-1}\log(t/2\pi)$, which exceeds $a/\pi$ exactly above $\Tst(a)$, so only the zeros above the horizon can sample $\PW_a$, and the floor measures how well a band-limited function can hide from them.

Both statements can be evaluated on the zeros themselves. The explicit formula is unconditional, and every zero used below lies on the line, so for the $K$-mode minimizer the sum over the zeros of $|F_K(\gamma)|^2$ must return $Q(f_K)=\lambda_K$ up to the contribution of the zeros above the last one used; this closes the loop on the object the form was built from.

\begin{conjecture}[tail law]\label{conj:tail}
Assume RH. For every $a>0$ that is not an entry, with $f$ the minimizer at support $a$ and $\lambda'$ the derivative of the floor,
\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}\;T\sum_{|\gamma|>T}|F(\gamma)|^2\;=\;-\frac{\lambda'(a)}{\pi}\,;\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \Phi'(a)=\pi\,T_{\mathrm{eff}}(a),\quad T_{\mathrm{eff}}(a)=\lim_{T\to\infty}T\vartheta(T).
\]
\end{conjecture}

The heuristic behind it is the edge law: a shape with $f(a-\delta)\sim C(\log(a/\delta))^{-1/2}$ has $|F(t)|^2\approx4C^2\cos^2(ta)/(t^2\log t)$ for large $t$, the zeros have density $\log(t/2\pi)/2\pi$, and the product integrated from $T$ gives $C^2/(\pi T)$, which is $-\lambda'/(2\pi T)$ by Conjecture~\ref{conj:hadamard}. The asymptotic expansion carries a ratio of logarithms, $\log(T/2\pi)/(\log(aT)+\beta)$, that would be $1.1$ to $1.4$ at the heights of Computation~\ref{comp:tail} and that the data do not show; the law is sharper than its derivation, which suggests a sum rule behind it. In the form $\Phi'=\pi T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ the two conjectures of Section~\ref{sec:conj} read: Conjecture~\ref{conj:A} is $T_{\mathrm{eff}}(a)\le(c/\pi)\Tst(a)$, the effective height of the zeros that carry the floor is a bounded multiple of the horizon; Conjecture~\ref{conj:B} is $T_{\mathrm{eff}}/\Tst\to c_0/\pi$, and the measured $c_0$ between $3.2$ and $3.9$ is $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ between $1.0$ and $1.24$ horizons.

The limit in Conjecture~\ref{conj:tail} is a theorem once the edge law is assumed with two derivatives.

\begin{proposition}[edge law implies tail law]\label{prop:tail}
Assume RH. Let $f$ be real, odd, supported in $[-a,a]$ and $C^2$ on $(-a,a)$, and suppose that for some $C>0$, $\beta\in\R$ and $\delta_0>0$,
\[
f(a-\delta)=C\big(\log(a/\delta)+\beta\big)^{-1/2}+\psi(\delta),\qquad 0<\delta<\delta_0,\qquad \psi^{(k)}(\delta)=o\big(\delta^{-k}\log^{-3/2}(1/\delta)\big)\ \ (k=0,1,2)\ \text{as }\delta\to0^+ .
\]
Then
\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}\;T\sum_{|\gamma|>T}|F(\gamma)|^2\;=\;\frac{2C^2}{\pi}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the transform.} Put $L(\delta)=\log(a/\delta)+\beta$, $\varphi(\delta)=CL(\delta)^{-1/2}$, so $\varphi'(\delta)=\tfrac C2L^{-3/2}\delta^{-1}>0$ and $|\varphi''(\delta)|\le C'L^{-3/2}\delta^{-2}$ on $(0,\delta_0)$. Let $\chi$ be smooth, $1$ on $[0,\delta_0/2]$ and $0$ beyond $\delta_0$; put $f_+(x)=(\varphi+\psi)(a-x)\chi(a-x)$ near the right edge, $f_-(x)=-f_+(-x)$, and $f_0=f-f_+-f_-$, which is $C^2$ with compact support in $(-a,a)$, so $F_0(t)=O(t^{-2})$. With $u=(\varphi+\psi)\chi$, $u(0^+)=u(\delta_0)=0$, one integration by parts gives $F_+(t)=e^{ita}\int_0^{\delta_0}u(\delta)e^{-it\delta}d\delta=e^{ita}(it)^{-1}\int_0^{\delta_0}u'(\delta)e^{-it\delta}d\delta$. In $u'=\varphi'\chi+\psi'\chi+(\varphi+\psi)\chi'$ the last term is smooth and supported away from $0$, contributing $O(1/t)$. For $J(t)=\int_0^{\delta_0}\varphi'\chi e^{-it\delta}d\delta$ split at $\delta=1/t$: on $[0,1/t]$, $e^{-it\delta}=1+O(t\delta)$ gives $\varphi(1/t)+O\big(t\int_0^{1/t}\varphi'\delta\,d\delta\big)=\varphi(1/t)+O(L(1/t)^{-3/2})$; on $[1/t,\delta_0]$ one more integration by parts gives $O(\varphi'(1/t)/t)+O\big(t^{-1}\int_{1/t}^{\delta_0}|(\varphi'\chi)'|\big)=O(L(1/t)^{-3/2})$. Hence $J(t)=CL(1/t)^{-1/2}(1+O(1/\log t))$. The same splitting applied to $\int\psi'\chi e^{-it\delta}$, with $\int_0^{1/t}|\psi'|=o(\log^{-1/2}t)$ and the hypothesis on $\psi''$, gives $o(\log^{-1/2}t)$. Therefore $F_+(t)=e^{ita}(it)^{-1}CL(1/t)^{-1/2}(1+O(1/\log t))$, and since $f$ is real and odd, $F_-(t)=-\overline{F_+(t)}=e^{-ita}(it)^{-1}CL(1/t)^{-1/2}(1+O(1/\log t))$. So
\[
F(t)=\frac{2C\cos(ta)}{it\sqrt{L(1/t)}}\big(1+O(1/\log t)\big)+O(t^{-2}),\qquad |F(t)|^2=\frac{4C^2\cos^2(ta)}{t^2L(1/t)}\big(1+O(1/\log t)\big)+O\big(t^{-3}\big).
\]
\emph{Step 2: the sum over the zeros.} Under RH the zeros are real and $N(t)=\frac t{2\pi}\log\frac t{2\pi e}+\frac78+S(t)+R(t)$ with $R$ smooth, $R=O(1/t)$, $R'=O(t^{-2})$, and, by Littlewood \cite[Ch.\ XIV, \S14.13]{titchmarsh}, $S(t)=O(\log t/\log\log t)$ and $S_1(t)=\int_0^tS=O(\log t/(\log\log t)^2)$. Put $w(t)=t^{-2}L(1/t)^{-1}$, $\rho(t)=\frac1{2\pi}\log\frac t{2\pi}$, so $\rho/L(1/t)\cdot t^2w=1+O(1/\log t)$. Since $\sum_{|\gamma|>T}=2\sum_{\gamma>T}$ and $4C^2\cos^2(ta)w=2C^2w(1+\cos2ta)$, the claim is $\sum_{\gamma>T}w(\gamma)=\frac1{2\pi T}(1+o(1))$, $\sum_{\gamma>T}\cos(2\gamma a)w(\gamma)=o(1/T)$, and $\sum_{\gamma>T}\big(w(\gamma)/\log\gamma+\gamma^{-3}\big)=o(1/T)$. For a positive decreasing $g$, $\sum_{\gamma>T}g(\gamma)=\int_T^\infty g\,dN=\int_T^\infty g\rho\,dt-g(T)S(T)-\int_T^\infty Sg'\,dt+\int_T^\infty gR'\,dt$, and with $|S|\ll\log t$ the last three terms are $O(g(T)\log T+\int_T^\infty|g'|\log t+\int_T^\infty g/t^2)$: for $g=w$ this is $O(\log T/T^2)=o(1/T)$ while $\int_T^\infty w\rho=\frac1{2\pi T}(1+O(1/\log T))$; for $g=w/\log t$ and $g=t^{-3}$ everything is $o(1/T)$. For the oscillatory sum put $G(t)=\cos(2ta)w(t)$: $\int_T^\infty G\rho\,dt=O(w(T)\rho(T)/a)=o(1/T)$ by one integration by parts against $\sin(2ta)/2a$, $\int_T^\infty GR'=o(1/T)$, and
\[
\int_T^\infty G\,dS=-G(T)S(T)+G'(T)S_1(T)+\int_T^\infty S_1G''\,dt,
\]
where $|G(T)S(T)|=O(\log T\,w(T))$, $|G'(T)S_1(T)|=O(a\log T\,w(T))$, and $|G''|\le4a^2w+O(w/t)$ gives $\int_T^\infty|S_1G''|=O\big(a^2\int_T^\infty\frac{\log t}{(\log\log t)^2t^2L(1/t)}dt\big)=O\big(\frac{a^2}{T(\log\log T)^2}\big)$, all $o(1/T)$. Hence $\sum_{|\gamma|>T}|F(\gamma)|^2=4C^2\cdot\frac1{2\pi T}(1+o(1))=\frac{2C^2}{\pi T}(1+o(1))$.
\end{proof}

With Conjecture~\ref{conj:hadamard}, $-\lambda'=2C^2$, the proposition is Conjecture~\ref{conj:tail}; its hypothesis is the edge law of Theorem~\ref{thm:edge} strengthened to an asymptotic equality with two derivatives, which is what Computation~\ref{comp:edge} measures and (H$_\infty$) does not give. RH enters twice, to read $Q$ as the sum over real zeros and through Littlewood's bound on $S_1$; without the latter the sum over the zeros is still $O(1/T)$, only the constant is lost. The $O(1/\log T)$ in the proof is the correction seen in the density integral of Computation~\ref{comp:tail}; that the zero sums show none of it at one percent is the sum rule still to be found. The Euler--Lagrange equation gives an exact identity for the tail at every height, in which the logarithms cancel.

\begin{proposition}[tail identity]\label{prop:tailid}
Let $f$ be a minimizer at support $a$ and let $K$ be the operator with symbol $\Psi$, so that $Q(f)=\ip{f}{Kf}-2P(f)^2$ and $f$ satisfies $Kf=\lambda f+2P(f)\,s$ on $(-a,a)$ with $s(y)=\sinh(y/2)\mathbf 1_{[-a,a]}(y)$. Put $g_{\mathrm{out}}=Kf\cdot\mathbf 1_{|y|>a}$, the force outside the window, and let $f_T$ be the low-pass of $f$ at height $T$, $\widehat{f_T}=F\mathbf 1_{|t|\le T}$. Then for every $T>0$
\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int_{|t|>T}|F(t)|^2\big(\Psi(t)-\lambda\big)\,dt\;=\;\frac{2P(f)}{2\pi}\int_{|t|>T}\overline{F(t)}\,\Sigma(t)\,dt\;-\;\int_{|y|>a}f_T(y)\,g_{\mathrm{out}}(y)\,dy,\qquad \Sigma=\hat s .
\]
Explicitly, for $|y|>a$, $g_{\mathrm{out}}(y)=-\int J_\Gamma(y-z)f(z)\,dz-\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\big(f(y-\log n)+f(y+\log n)\big)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the whole line $Kf=\lambda f+2Ps+g_{\mathrm{out}}$, since the window equation holds inside and $g_{\mathrm{out}}$ is by definition the value of $Kf$ outside. In Fourier form, $(\Psi-\lambda)F=2P\Sigma+G$ with $G=\widehat{g_{\mathrm{out}}}$. Multiply by $\overline F\mathbf 1_{|t|>T}$ and integrate: the term with $G$ is $\frac1{2\pi}\int_{|t|>T}\overline FG=\ip{f-f_T}{g_{\mathrm{out}}}$ by Parseval, and $\ip f{g_{\mathrm{out}}}=0$ because the supports are disjoint. The formula for $g_{\mathrm{out}}$ is the kernel of $K$ off the diagonal: from \eqref{eq:Winf}, $(K_\infty f)(y)=c_0f(y)+\int_0^\infty\big[2f(y)e^{-2x}-(f(y-x)+f(y+x))e^{-x/2}\big](1-e^{-2x})^{-1}dx$, which for $f(y)=0$ is $-\int J_\Gamma(y-z)f(z)\,dz$, and the prime part of $Q$ is the operator $-\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}(\tau_{\log n}+\tau_{-\log n})$. Both sides converge: $g_{\mathrm{out}}$ is smooth away from $\pm a$, decays exponentially, and by the edge law grows like $-C(\log(1/\delta))^{1/2}$ at distance $\delta$ outside the edge, while $f_T$ is bounded and $O(1/|y|)$.
\end{proof}

The identity is the mechanism of the tail law. Just outside the edge, $g_{\mathrm{out}}(a+\delta)=-C\,L(\delta)^{1/2}(1+o(1))$ with $L(\delta)=\log(a/\delta)+\beta$ (the kernel $1/(2|s|)$ acting on the edge profile $CL^{-1/2}$), and the low-pass of $f$ leaks outside the edge as $f_T(a+\delta)=C\,L(1/T)^{-1/2}\,\pi^{-1}\big(\tfrac\pi2-\operatorname{Si}(T\delta)\big)(1+o(1))$; their overlap is $2C^2/(\pi T)$ times $\int_0^\infty(\tfrac\pi2-\operatorname{Si})=1$, with $L^{-1/2}L^{1/2}=1$: no logarithm, and it is the tail law, since the left side is $\int_{|t|>T}|F|^2\rho\,(1+o(1))$. The remaining pieces, the overlap with the smooth part of $g_{\mathrm{out}}$ (the regular part of the kernel, the other edge, the reflected primes) and the polar integral, are each of order $C\,L(1/T)^{-1/2}/T$, larger than $C^2/T$ when $C$ is small, and must cancel. \begin{heuristic}[why the effective height is tied to the horizon]\label{heur:why}
Below the horizon the zeros are sparser than the $a/\pi$ degrees of freedom per unit height of a function of type $a$, and the minimizer vanishes at all of them: their share of the floor is $10^{-5}$, $10^{-7}$, $10^{-13}$ at $a=0.6,0.8,1.0$. Above it, the excess of the zero density over the Nyquist density is $\log(t/\Tst)/2\pi$, the same function of $t/\Tst$ for every $a$; so, after scaling heights by the horizon, the minimizer faces the same problem at every support, which is why $T_{\mathrm{eff}}/\Tst$ should tend to a constant, and why the profiles $\vartheta(T/\Tst)$ should converge as $a$ grows. The only way for a function of type $a$ to be small at more points than it has freedom for is to steepen at the edge (the edge law and the Hadamard formula), and the edge amplitude feeds the tail at every height at once, at the rate $C^2/(\pi T)$; the minimizer balances the gain from hiding at one more zero just above the horizon against that cost, and the balance falls at a fixed multiple of $\Tst$. The spare freedom below the horizon is worth $\Tst/2\pi$ zeros, and the accumulated excess above it, $\int_{\Tst}^T\log(t/\Tst)\,dt/2\pi$, exhausts it at $T=e\Tst$; the measured median heights are below that and rising with $a$. Two things enter that no counting supplies: positivity of every term, which is RH, and the absence of gaps between zeros wider than the resolution $\pi/a$ above a few horizons, in which a shape could park its tail. The explanation is therefore conditional on where the zeros are; it says what Conjecture~\ref{conj:A} asserts about them, not why the prime side must produce it.
\end{heuristic}

The entries have a zero-side reading in the same picture. The tail of the floor carries $\cos^2(\gamma a)=\tfrac12(1+\cos2\gamma a)$ over the zeros, so it contains the average of $\cos(2\gamma a)$ over the tail-weighted zeros, and by the explicit formula the zeros' rotations align exactly when $2a=\log n$.

\begin{computation}[the zeros' rotation at the entries]\label{comp:align}
\texttt{rh\_alignment.py}, first $6000$ zeros: $R(a)=\sum_\gamma\cos(2\gamma a)\,w(\gamma)/\sum_\gamma w(\gamma)$ with the tail weight $w=\gamma^{-2}\log^{-1}(\gamma/2\pi)$, on $0.3\le a\le1.3$ in steps of $0.0005$. Its mean absolute value is $0.27$ and its $95$th percentile $0.53$; at the entries of the primes it is $-0.53$ ($a_2$), $-0.62$ ($a_3$, the minimum over the whole range, at $a=0.5495$), $-0.57$ ($a_5$), $-0.56$ ($a_7$), $-0.44$ ($a_{11}$), each minimum within $0.0005$ of $a_n$; at the prime powers $4,8,9$, whose weights $\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}=0.35,0.25,0.37$ are below the noise of this truncation, it is $+0.10,-0.08,+0.22$. Around $a_3$ the curve is a cusp: with the zeros above $3\Tst$ only and weight $\gamma^{-2}$, $R$ is $-0.097,-0.158,-0.226,-0.161,-0.103$ at offsets $-0.004,-0.002,0,+0.002,+0.004$, linear flanks meeting at the entry. A cusp in the tail mass is a jump in $\lambda'$: this is the kink of Proposition~\ref{prop:kink} seen from the zeros, and with the edge law in place of a jump it is the soft kink of \eqref{eq:soft}. The relay, the soft kink, the sawtooth of Computation~\ref{comp:relay} and the tail law are one mechanism: the zeros rotate with the window, align at the primes, and the alignment is felt through the tail that the edge feeds.
\end{computation}

\begin{heuristic}[prolate lower bound]\label{heur:prolate}
The number of zeros in $[-\Tst,\Tst]$ is $2N(\Tst)\approx(\Tst/\pi)(2a-1)$, short of the Shannon number $2a\Tst/\pi$ of $[-a,a]\times[-\Tst,\Tst]$ by $\Tst/\pi$. By the Landau--Pollak--Slepian theory \cite{slepian-pollak,lps}, with the asymptotics of the prolate spectrum in the plunge region \cite{slepian65,landau-widom} (about $(2/\pi^2)\log c$ eigenvalues between $\varepsilon$ and $1-\varepsilon$ for $c=a\Tst$), a function in $\PW_a$ constrained to vanish on all zeros below the horizon can still keep all but $\eta\gtrsim\exp(-\pi\Tst/(\log(a\Tst)+O(1)))$ of its energy below the horizon; under RH this bounds $\lambda$ from above, i.e.\ $\Phi\gtrsim\pi\Tst/\log(a\Tst)$, which is $0.7\,\Tst$ at $a=1$. The observed value is $\Phi(1)=59.5=1.28\,\Tst$ (Computation~\ref{comp:grid}); the zeros are about twice as expensive a constraint set as the count alone predicts, and the difference is arithmetic. Conjecture~\ref{conj:A} is the matching upper bound.
\end{heuristic}

\section{The boundary law}\label{sec:hadamard}

The derivative of the floor should be a boundary quantity. Since $Q$ does not depend on $a$, $\lambda(a)$ is the infimum of a fixed form over the growing family $L^2(-a,a)$, and its derivative is a domain variation. For the Dirichlet fractional Laplacian, Hadamard's formula expresses such a derivative through the boundary trace $u/\mathrm{dist}^s$ of the eigenfunction \cite{djitte-fall-weth}; for the logarithmic Laplacian the boundary trace is the amplitude $C$ of Computation~\ref{comp:edge}. A Pohozaev identity for $L_\Delta$ with the local boundary term $\int_{\partial\Omega}u^2\ln(\delta^{-2})\,(x\cdot\nu)$ was claimed in \cite{agv}, which its authors withdrew on 26 April 2025 ``due to a crucial error in Proof of Pohozaev identity''; to our knowledge no correct version exists. We therefore state the law as a conjecture and give the evidence.

\begin{conjecture}[boundary law]\label{conj:hadamard}
Assume (H$_\infty$). For $a$ not an entry, with $C_\pm(a)=\lim_{\delta\to0}\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}\,|f(\pm(a-\delta))|$ the edge amplitudes of the minimizer (assumed to exist),
\[
\lambda'(a)=-\big(C_+(a)^2+C_-(a)^2\big)=-2\,C(a)^2 .
\]
Equivalently, with Proposition~\ref{prop:dini}, the Weil form on a window satisfies the Pohozaev-type identity
$\mathcal D(f)=2a\,C(a)^2$ on its minimizer.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{computation}[the boundary law]\label{comp:hadamard}
$C$ from the edge FEM (two-stage mesh to $10^{-12}a$, fit of $1/f^2$ against $\log(a/\delta)$ on $14\le t\le20$, residual $\le5\times10^{-5}$, mesh bias $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-2}$ in $C$) and $\lambda_{48}'$ from \texttt{rh\_dilation.py} (Hellmann--Feynman, ball arithmetic, $48$ modes; the truncation error of $\lambda_{48}'$ is below $10^{-4}$ at these supports by the $K$-stability of Computation~\ref{comp:relay}); data in \texttt{boundary\_law.csv} and \texttt{data/}, reproduced by \texttt{rh\_hadamard.py}:

An exactly solvable case: for one half of the Dirichlet form of $L_\Delta$ on $(-a,a)$, the scaling identity $L_\Delta(u(\cdot/a))=(L_\Delta u)(\cdot/a)-2\log a\,u(\cdot/a)$ gives $\lambda(a)=\lambda(1)-\log a$, so the law predicts $C(a)^2=1/(2a)$. The prime-free form differs from this by the bounded corrections (the polar term and $\Ree\psi-\log|t|$); its amplitudes give $2aC^2=1.028,\,1.066,\,1.129,\,1.223$ at $a=0.3,\dots,0.6$ and its derivatives $-a\lambda'=1.029,\,1.067,\,1.129,\,1.223$, both near $1$ and equal to each other, which is the content of the table restated, not an independent test.

\begin{remark}[consequence of Conjecture~\ref{conj:hadamard}]
$\Phi'(a)=2C(a)^2/\lambda(a)$, and Conjecture~\ref{conj:A} becomes
\[
C(a)^2\le\tfrac{c}{2}\,\Tst(a)\,\lambda(a)\qquad(a\ge a_0):
\]
the edge amplitude of the minimizer is bounded by its energy times the horizon. This is the form in which the conjecture is a statement about one function at one place. The relay of Computation~\ref{comp:relay} reads: as $a$ approaches an entry from below, $\lambda$ falls toward the deleted form's failure point while $C$ stays of the same order, and $\Phi'=2C^2/\lambda$ rises until the entry rescues the floor. Under the Riemann Hypothesis, $C(a)$ is the boundary trace of the band-limited function that best hides from the zeros above the horizon.
\end{remark}

\section{Relation to the literature, and acknowledgement}\label{sec:lit}

Suzuki \cite{suzuki-screw} organizes the Weil form on $[-a,a]$ as a family of self-adjoint operators and conjectures that an operator with the zeros as spectrum is their limit as $a\to\infty$; Conjecture~\ref{conj:A} is a differential inequality for the ground state of that family. Zhu \cite{zhu} certifies both parity sectors at $a=0.8$; his Landau--Widom decay law is fitted to the even-sector floor, calibrated on $L\le1.2$ and validated at $L=1.4,\dots,2.0$, and his exploratory $950$-mode value at $L=1.19$, between $10^{-48}$ and $10^{-46}$, is for the full space of test functions, i.e.\ the even floor; the odd floors of Section~\ref{sec:grid} interpolate to $\lambda(1.19)\approx3\times10^{-44}$, and the even floor is already three orders below the odd one at $a=0.8$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:zhu}), so the two are consistent. Comparisons between his law and the odd $\Phi'$ are cross-sector and are labelled so. Connes--Consani \cite{connes-consani} treat the range $a\le\tfrac12\log2$ where only the archimedean place acts; the wall of Proposition~\ref{prop:wall} is that place acting alone at the edge for every $a$. The atlas \cite{michaels-atlas} contains an exact-rational certificate of the odd floor to $a=3/8$ with constant $1/200$, inside Zhu's range, and the first application of Theorem~\ref{thm:edge} to this operator. The folds paper \cite{michaels-folds} computed the floors to $a=1.1$, the deleted-form failure points and the symbol's negative set, and described the relay as a sawtooth, which Computation~\ref{comp:relay} measures; the M\"obius companion \cite{michaels-mobius} treats the Green-energy side of the same program.

Computation~\ref{comp:zeros} is the converse use of the explicit formula: the form was built from the primes and the archimedean symbol, and evaluating the minimizer on the actual zeros returns the floor and its derivative; with Computations~\ref{comp:identity} and the Fourier-side check the identity has now been measured from the matrices, from the symbol and from the zeros. An independent referee report on the first version of this paper identified the gaps corrected here: the identity had been asserted for the exact form on numerical grounds (now Theorem~\ref{thm:identity} for the $K$-mode form and Proposition~\ref{prop:dini} for the exact one), Conjecture~\ref{conj:A} had been phrased in terms of $\Phi$ and so presupposed $\lambda>0$, the abstract overstated several computations, the resolution threshold had been misattributed, the scaling identity carried a sign error, \eqref{eq:soft} lacked the constant $1-\log2$, and Computation~\ref{comp:grid} misdescribed its data; the report's own recomputations of every table from the data files are gratefully acknowledged.
